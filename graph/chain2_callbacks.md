# Chain 2 — Model-Then-Extract

Teacher-facing callback script for the year's second through-line. Archetype: **given a working model, solve for the specific input that makes the output match a target.** Four canonical items, four lessons, four function families (polynomial · rational · exponential decay · exponential compound).

Chain 2 pairs with Chain 1 as the complementary half of Algebra 2's modeling story:

- **Chain 1** (derive-from-constraint) = *Build* the model from a constraint, then invert it.
- **Chain 2** (model-then-extract) = The model is *given*. Solve for a specific value.

Students meet Chain 1 first (Storage Box, April). Chain 2 hands them the opposite half: *sometimes you don't build the machine, you just operate it.*

---

## 1. Lesson 3-5 P3 — Venetta Franchises `3-5-q30` (DOK-3, polynomial modeling, APRIL)

**The plant.** First appearance of the archetype. Chain 1 was already planted earlier in the same lesson (Storage Box), so this is the second DOK-3 move students meet in 3-5.

> *"Storage Box at the start of today asked you to **build** a polynomial from what the box looked like. Venetta is doing the reverse: we're **given** a profit polynomial — someone else already did the modeling work — and now we're operating it. We want a specific answer: in what year did profit first hit $X? The polynomial is the machine; we're the operator. Notice both moves exist in the world, and notice they feel different."*

Say this aloud. The move-differentiation is the entire point of stacking both DOK-3 items in one lesson.

---

## 2. Lesson 4-5 P1 — Chemist Mixture `4-5-q33` (DOK-3, rational modeling)

**The first callback.** Still a single prior item; keep it light.

> *"Remember Venetta? Someone handed us a profit polynomial and we solved for a specific year. Today the chemist hands us a rational equation — the one that relates alcohol percentage to gallons added — and we solve for a specific amount. Same move, different function family. The equation is **given**; we **operate** it."*

*Tip:* the extraneous-solution check at the end of Chemist is shared structure with Chain 5 (extraneous chain). You can run a side-note: "this is where Chain 2 and the extraneous chain cross."

---

## 3. Lesson 5-4 P2 — Soft-Drink Half-Life `5-4-q41` (DOK-3, exponential-decay modeling)

**Peak cross-representational callback.** Third family, same move.

> *"Two prior versions. Venetta was polynomial: profit as a cubic. Chemist was rational: a fraction equation. Today is exponential decay: y equals 50 times one-half to the t-over-5. The model is **given** again — someone handed us the 5-hour half-life — and we solve for the specific t where concentration hits a target. **Same question every time: for what input does the machine give this output?**"*

*Optional move:* after solving, ask **"what's the half-life if the function were y = 50·(0.5)^(t/8)?"** — forces students to notice the parameter is the variable that changes the machine's behavior, not the target.

---

## 4. Lesson 6-3 quick — Continuous Compound `6-3-q52` (DOK-2, log-enabled extraction)

**Closure callback — fourth family, logs enter as the tool.**

> *"We've done this three times now — polynomial, rational, exponential decay. Today: continuous compound, y equals P times e to the rt. The model is **given**, we solve for t — same as always. What's new is that to invert e-to-the-t we need a new tool, the natural log. **That's why this unit exists** — to give us the lock-pick for exponential equations. Every time we've done model-then-extract, we needed an inverse operation. Today's inverse is ln. Same move, fifth representation, new tool."*

This is the line that makes students feel the unit is *earning its keep* rather than being a new abstract topic.

---

## Interplay with Chain 1 — the handoff at 6-5

Chain 1 ends and Chain 2 takes over at `6-5-q40` Part C (Richter generalization). That item is structurally Chain 2 (given a model, extract a pattern) but it closes the Richter → logs narrative opened by Chain 1.

When you land 6-5-q40 Part C:

> *"We've done **two** kinds of DOK-3 work this year. Chain 1 was build-and-invert: Storage Box, Cylinder, Revenue. Chain 2 was operate-the-given-machine: Venetta, Chemist, Half-Life, Compound Interest. Today, **Richter asks you to do both at once** — the formula is given (Chain 2), but Part C asks you to generalize the pattern, which is the same mental move as building a constraint (Chain 1). This is the year in one problem."*

Name it. The student who hears this will file the year as coherent rather than episodic.

---

## Teacher meta-guidance

- **Chain 2 runs "lighter" than Chain 1.** Chain 1's items are almost all DOK-3 and get dedicated class time. Chain 2 has one DOK-2 item (Compound Interest) and doesn't need the same fanfare — a single sentence suffices.
- **The Venetta / Chemist / Half-Life / Compound trio is the year's "word problem" backbone.** If students struggle with Chain 2, they will struggle on the assessment. Chain 1 is about depth; Chain 2 is about transfer.
- **Together, Chains 1 and 2 are the entire DOK-3 expectation for the year.** Prove-by-properties (Chain 6) and read-from-representation (currently cold) are both thin by comparison. That tells you what kind of mathematician the Savvas Algebra 2 curriculum is shaping: a **modeler**, not a prover.

## If period pacing collapses

If you lose one of the four items to time, drop **Compound Interest** (6-3-q52) — it's the DOK-2 member. Keep Venetta → Chemist → Half-Life as the irreducible trunk (three DOK-3 items across three different function families).
