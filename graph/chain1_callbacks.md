# Chain 1 — Extract-Hidden-Variable

Teacher-facing callback script for the year's strongest echo chain. The underlying archetype: **given a physical or geometric relationship, find the hidden variable by inverting the model.** Seven items, six lessons, five representations (polynomial · inverse variation · rational exponent · log · exponential inverse).

The point of saying these lines aloud isn't the line itself — it's forcing the student to notice that the *move* is the same even though the *machinery* changes. That reframes Algebra 2 from "eleven isolated lesson topics" to "one idea with a growing vocabulary."

---

## 1. Lesson 3-5 P3 — Storage Box `3-5-q27` (DOK-3, derive-from-constraint, APRIL)

**The plant.** First appearance of the archetype — nothing to call back to yet.

> *"Notice what we just did: we had a physical object, we had one constraint (volume equals something), and we had to pull out a hidden dimension. That move — **build a polynomial from a constraint, then extract a dimension** — is a year-long move. We're going to see it again with a milk container in May, with a money formula in June. Today's work is how we learn the machine. Same move, new machine each time."*

---

## 2. Lesson 4-1 P2 — Wavelength / Frequency `4-1-q17` (DOK-2, inverse variation)

**The first callback.** Easy lift — the same "given constraint, find the hidden partner" move with different mathematics.

> *"Remember Storage Box? We had a volume constraint, we wanted a dimension. Here we have a **product constraint** — wavelength times frequency equals the speed of light — and we still want to pull out one side given the other. Different equation, same job."*

*Secondary plant:* this is where inverse variation becomes the thematic bridge between polynomial modeling (Topic 3) and the reciprocal/log families (Topics 4–6). Name it.

---

## 3. Lesson 5-1 quick — Cylinder Capstone `5-1-q50` (DOK-3, derive-from-constraint, RATIONAL EXPONENT)

**The strongest callback in the chain.** Two prior examples to reach back to.

> *"Storage Box in April. Wavelength-and-frequency in May. Today it's a milk cylinder. In all three: a physical object, a given constraint, a hidden dimension. What's new today — and what makes this a **rational-exponent** problem, not a polynomial one — is that the radius comes out under a cube root. We'll need the new tool from last week (rational exponents) to finish the same move we've been doing all year."*

*Optional deepening:* after solving, ask **"what would this look like if we knew the radius and wanted the volume?"** — reverses the inverse move, reinforces that the direction of extraction is the choice, not a mathematical necessity.

---

## 4. Lesson 6-3 quick — Richter Magnitude `6-3-q54` (DOK-2, log modeling)

**The representation shift.** Same move, first appearance in the log family. Worth a pause.

> *"Here's the move one more time: given a formula tying two quantities together (amplitude A and magnitude R), we want to extract one given the other. The difference: this time the link between them is a logarithm, not a polynomial or a cube root. We're going to spend this lesson learning that logs can live inside the same move we've been practicing since Storage Box."*

---

## 5. Lesson 6-4 — Revenue Inverse `6-4-q28` (DOK-3, derive-from-constraint, EXPONENTIAL INVERSE)

**The peak callback — five prior instances, all the same archetype.**

> *"We've done this five times now: Storage Box, wavelength, cylinder, Richter, and today revenue. Every time: a model, a constraint, a hidden input we want to recover. Today's twist: the original model is **exponential**, so to invert it we need a **logarithm**. That's why we spent yesterday on logs. A log isn't a new topic — it's the specific tool that lets us invert an exponential model. Same job we've been doing all year, new lock, new key."*

---

## 6. Lesson 6-4 — Telescope Magnitude `6-4-q33` (DOK-2, reinforcement)

**Micro-callback.** Don't belabor — just name the pattern.

> *"Another physical formula, another extraction. At this point you should be able to see the shape from a mile away — constraint plus inversion."*

---

## 7. Lesson 6-5 quick — Richter Generalization `6-5-q40` Part C (DOK-2, model-then-generalize)

**The capstone callback — also a flavor handoff.** This item closes Chain 1 and opens into the model-then-extract flavor (Chain 2).

> *"We started the year extracting a specific dimension from a specific object. Here we extract a **pattern**: not 'what's the magnitude when amplitude is 150 times reference,' but **'what does the magnitude do for any amplitude multiple?'** That's the same move one level up — finding structure, not just a number. If Storage Box taught us to extract a length, this teaches us to extract a law."*

---

## Teacher meta-guidance

- **Say the previous items by name.** "Storage Box" and "Cylinder Capstone" are proper nouns in your classroom — they are memorable the way "the one with the box" is not.
- **Don't ask students to recite.** The callback is a one-way narrative device. Make the connection; let them hear it. Comprehension reveals itself in whether they attack the new problem faster.
- **Four minutes is enough.** These lines shouldn't become mini-lessons. The point is continuity, not teaching by analogy.
- **When a student notices the pattern unprompted, that's the win.** Flag it in your notes — that student is ready for Chain 2 (model-then-extract: Venetta → Chemist → Half-life → Compound Interest) to be made explicit the same way.

## Open adaptation

If the real-classroom pacing collapses one of the Topic 6 items (short period, skipped section), the chain still holds — Storage Box → Cylinder → Revenue Inverse is the irreducible trunk. Don't cut below three.
