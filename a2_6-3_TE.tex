\begin{te-addendum}{0}{GeneralizeETP}
\textbf{Lesson Overview: FOCUS}
Objective. Students will be able to:
\item Understand the inverse relationship between exponents and logarithms.
\item Use logarithms to solve exponential models.
\item Evaluate logarithms using technology.
Essential Understanding. A logarithmic function is the inverse of an exponential function. Logarithms are found by determining the exponent that must be applied to a base to yield a given result.
\textbf{COHERENCE}
Previously in this topic, students: Interpreted the key features of exponential functions. Used exponential functions to model relationships.
In this lesson, students: Learn about the relationship between exponents and logarithms and use this relationship to solve problems. Evaluate common and natural logarithms and solve equations involving logarithms.
Increasing Coherence. Examples 5 and 6 are not necessarily expected to be addressed on high stakes assessments.
Later in this topic students will: Evaluate logarithmic expressions using properties of logarithms.
\textbf{RIGOR}
This lesson emphasizes a blend of conceptual understanding and procedural skill and fluency. Students practice evaluating logarithms to understand the inverse relationship between exponents and logarithms.

Taylor and Chen used different methods to find the magnitude of the earthquake with amplitude 5,500.
Taylor: 5,500 is halfway between 1,000 and 10,000. 3.5 is halfway between 3 and 4. The magnitude is about 3.5.
Chen: (y=10^x)
(10^3=1,000)
(10^4=10,000)

\begin{example}{1}
\textbf{Understand Logarithms}
CONCEPTUAL UNDERSTANDING. Solve (2^x=8).
The operation in (2^x=8) is exponentiation. To solve this equation, you need an inverse operation that answers the question, ``To what exponent would you raise the base 2 to get 8?''
\te{Callout: Division is the inverse of multiplication, so you can divide both sides by 2 to solve the equation. (\frac{2x}{2}=\frac82); (x=4).}
A logarithm is the inverse of exponentiation. To solve the equation (2^x=8), you can write (\log_2 8=x). Solving this gives (\log_2 8=3) because (2^3=8).
\te{Callout: This is read ``logarithm base 2 of 8'' or ``log base 2 of 8.''}
\te{USE STRUCTURE. Creating the notation (\log_2 x) to represent the exponent to which you raise 2 to get (x) is similar to creating the radical notation (\sqrt{x}) to represent one number you can square to get (x).}
\answer{(\log_2 8=3) because (2^3=8).}
\end{example}
\begin{te-addendum}{1}{GeneralizeETP}
\textbf{Establish Mathematics Goals to Focus Learning}
Q: How is solving (2^x=8) similar to solving (2x=8)? different?
\answer{Similar: both use inverse operations; different: inverse operation used to solve (2x=8) is division and inverse operation used to solve (2^x=8) is a logarithm.}
Q: You can calculate the value of (x) in (2^x=8) without rewriting the function as (\log_2 8=x). When might it be necessary to use this notation?
\answer{It will be necessary to use the inverse form of an exponential function to solve more difficult problems.}
\end{te-addendum}

\begin{te-addendum}{1}{GeneralizeETP}
\textbf{EXAMPLE 1 CONTINUED}
The logarithm base (b) of (x) is defined as follows.
(\log_b x=y) if and only if (b^y=x), for (b>0), (b\neq1), and (x>0).
The logarithmic function (y=\log_b x) is the inverse of the exponential function (y=b^x).
\end{te-addendum}
\begin{tryit}{1}
Write the logarithmic form of (y=8^x).
\answer{(x=\log_8 y)}
\end{tryit}
\begin{concept-box}
\textbf{Exponential and Logarithmic Forms}
Exponential form shows that a base raised to an exponent equals the result.
(a^b=c)
Logarithmic form shows that the log of the result with the given base equals the exponent.
(\log_a c=b)
\te{Callout: When written in logarithmic form, the number that was the result of the exponential equation is often called the argument. Color coding: base red, exponent green, result purple.}
\end{concept-box}
\begin{example}{2}
\textbf{Convert Between Exponential and Logarithmic Forms}
A. What is the logarithmic form of (3^4=81)?
The base is 3, the exponent is 4, and the result is 81.
So, in logarithmic form,
(3^4=81\rightarrow\log_3 81=4).
The logarithmic form of (3^4=81) is (\log_3 81=4).
\answer{(\log_3 81=4)}
B. What is the exponential form of (\log_{10}1,000=3)?
The base is 10, the exponent is 3, and the result (or argument) is 1,000.
So, in exponential form,
(\log_{10}1,000=3\rightarrow10^3=1,000).
The exponential form of (\log_{10}1,000=3) is (10^3=1,000).
\answer{(10^3=1,000)}
\te{STUDY TIP. A fact family for 2, 3, and 5 are the four addition and subtraction equations that relate the three numbers. Think of exponential and logarithmic forms as a fact family for the three numbers given.}
\end{example}

\textbf{Evaluate Logarithms}
What is the value of each logarithmic expression?
A. (\log_5 125)
THINK: (5^{?}=125)

\answer{3}
B. (\log_{\frac14}16)

\begin{concept-box}
\textbf{Common Logarithms and Natural Logarithms}
The base 10 logarithm is called the common logarithm and is written as (\log x) with the base of 10 implied.
The base (e) logarithm is called the natural logarithm and is written as (\ln x).
\end{concept-box}
\begin{example}{4}
\textbf{Evaluate Common and Natural Logarithms}
What is the value of each logarithmic expression to the nearest ten-thousandth?
A. (\log900)
(\log900\approx2.9542)
(10^{2.9542}\approx900)
\answer{2.9542}
B. (\ln e)
(\ln e=1)
(e^1=e)
\answer{1}
C. (\ln(-1.87))
(\ln(-1.87)\rightarrow\text{ERROR})
(e^{?}=-1.87)
There is no exponent to which (e) can be raised in order to get a negative number, so (\ln(-1.87)) is undefined.
\answer{undefined}

\begin{te-addendum}{5}{GeneralizeETP}
\textbf{Facilitate Meaningful Mathematical Discourse}
Q: How is solving exponential and logarithmic equations similar to solving linear equations? How is it different?
\answer{Solving any type of equation requires the use of inverse operations. To solve linear equations, you use the inverse operations of addition/subtraction and multiplication/division. To solve exponential equations, you use logarithms, and vice versa.}
\end{te-addendum}
\begin{example}{6}
\textbf{Use Logarithms to Solve Problems}
APPLICATION. The seismic energy, (x), in joules can be estimated based on the magnitude, (m), of an earthquake by the formula (x=10^{1.5m+12}). What is the magnitude of an earthquake with a seismic energy of (4.2\times10^{20}) joules?
Formulate. Substitute (4.2\times10^{20}) for (x) in the formula.
(4.2\times10^{20}=10^{1.5m+12})
Compute. Solve the equation for (m).
(4.2\times10^{20}=10^{1.5m+12})
(\log(4.2\times10^{20})=1.5m+12)
(20.6\approx1.5m+12)
(5.75\approx m)
\te{Callout: Rewrite in logarithmic form.}
Interpret. The magnitude of the earthquake is about 5.75.
Verify the answer: (10^{1.5(5.75)+12}\approx4.2\times10^{20}).
\answer{The magnitude of the earthquake is about 5.75.}

Q: Explain the relationship between logarithms and exponents.
\answer{Logarithms and exponents are inverse operations.}
Q: When is it useful to convert between exponential and logarithmic forms?
\answer{It is useful to rewrite an exponential equation in logarithmic form when the exponent contains a variable. The logarithm can be evaluated and the resulting linear equation can easily be solved. Similarly, it is useful to rewrite a logarithmic equation in exponential form when the result (or argument) contains a variable. The exponent, with a base of 10 or e can be evaluated and the resulting linear equation can easily be solved.}
\end{te-addendum}
