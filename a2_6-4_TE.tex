\begin{te-addendum}{0}{GeneralizeETP}
\textbf{Lesson Overview: FOCUS}
Objective. Students will be able to:
\item Graph logarithmic functions and interpret their key features.
\item Write and interpret the inverses of exponential and logarithmic functions.
Essential Understanding. The inverse relationship between exponential and logarithmic functions reveals key features of the graphs of both functions. Logarithmic functions can be used to model several real-world situations.
\textbf{COHERENCE}
Previously in this topic, students: Wrote the inverse of an exponential function using logarithmic notation.
In this lesson, students: Extend their understanding of the inverse relationship between exponential and logarithmic functions by finding key features of logarithmic graphs, as well as interpreting logarithmic models and their inverses.
Increasing Coherence. Examples 3 and 4 are not necessarily expected to be addressed on high stakes assessments.
Later in this topic, students will: Use their knowledge of properties of exponents, combined with their understanding of the inverse relationship between exponents and logarithms, to identify properties of logarithms.
\textbf{RIGOR}
This lesson emphasizes a blend of procedural skill and fluency and application.
\item Students use key features of logarithmic functions to graph transformations of the parent function.
\item Students interpret and compare logarithmic functions as a means for understanding models that relate advertising costs to revenue and models of plane altitudes.
\end{te-addendum}

Students graph logarithmic functions and identify domain, range, x-intercept, asymptote, and end behavior of the graphs. They graph transformations of logarithmic functions and compare the key features to the graph of the parent function.
Students write and interpret the inverse of a formula involving logarithms. They compare the key features of two logarithmic functions in the context of a real-world situation.
\textbf{Applying Math Practices}
Model with Mathematics. Students use equations and graphs to describe and interpret the results of mathematical situations such as advertising costs and airplane altitudes.
Look For and Make Use of Structure. Students apply general mathematical rules of transforming functions to transform graphs of logarithmic functions. They also use structure by identifying that because logarithmic functions are inverses of exponential functions, their graphs are reflections of exponential functions across the line (y=x).

\begin{example}{4}
\textbf{Use a Logarithmic Function as a Model}
A logarithmic function can approximate the altitude of a plane over time.
% FIG 54 982 327 1156 445
\placeholder{illustration}{Plane ascending from runway beside control tower, curved black rising path and vertical double-headed arrow from ground to plane labeled A is altitude in feet. Callout A=9,200 ln t+10,000, and t is time in minutes after take off. Blue sky and gray runway.}
A. What is the altitude of the plane 10 minutes after takeoff?
Substitute 10 for t in the equation of the function.
(A=9,200\ln 10+10,000)
(\approx9,200(2.303)+10,000)
(\approx21,188+10,000)
(\approx31,188)
After 10 minutes, the plane's altitude is about 31,188 feet.
\answer{about 31,188 feet}
B. How long after takeoff will the plane reach an altitude of 35,000 feet?
Rearrange the equation to isolate the variable t.
(A=9,200\ln t+10,000)
(A-10,000=9,200\ln t)
(\frac{A-10,000}{9,200}=\ln t)
(t=e^{\frac{A-10,000}{9,200}})
In the rearranged equation, time is a function of altitude, so use substitution to find time.
(t=e^{\frac{35,000-10,000}{9,200}})
(\approx15.14)
The plane will reach an altitude of 35,000 feet after about 15.14 minutes.
\answer{about 15.14 minutes}
\te{STUDY TIP: The original equation is a logarithmic function, while the rearranged equation is an exponential function. The exponential function is the inverse of the logarithmic function.}
C. Evaluate the model against the altitude of the plane.
On the domain interval (0,0.337) the logarithmic function is negative. The altitude of the plane is not correct during this interval. Also, the plane starts at the origin, which is on the asymptote of the model.
\answer{On the domain interval (0,0.337) the logarithmic function is negative. The altitude of the plane is not correct during this interval. Also, the plane starts at the origin, which is on the asymptote of the model.}
\end{example}
