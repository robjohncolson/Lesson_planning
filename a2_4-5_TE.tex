\begin{te-addendum}{0}{GeneralizeETP}
\textbf{Lesson Overview: FOCUS}
Objective. Students will be able to:
\item Solve rational equations in one variable.
\item Identify extraneous solutions to rational equations and give examples of how they arise.
Essential Understanding. Rational equations contain a rational expression and can be solved by multiplying each side of the equation by a common denominator to eliminate the fractions. Any solution that is excluded from the domain of the original equation is extraneous.
\textbf{COHERENCE}
Previously in this topic, students:
\item Added, subtracted, multiplied, and divided rational expressions.
In this lesson, students:
\item Solve rational equations by multiplying each side of the equation by a common denominator and identifying extraneous solutions.
In later topics, students will:
\item Add, subtract, multiply, and divide rational expressions as part of the process for solving exponential and logarithmic equations.
\textbf{RIGOR}
This lesson emphasizes a blend of procedural skill and fluency and application.
\item Students solve rational equations by multiplying each side of the equation by a common denominator to eliminate fractions.
\item Students apply their knowledge of operations with rational expressions to solve rate problems.
\end{te-addendum}

\textbf{Mathematics Overview}
Students find solutions to rational equations in one variable. They multiply both sides of the equation by a common denominator and then solve for the variable. Students also find extraneous solutions by identifying values that are excluded from the domain of the original equation.
\textbf{Applying Math Practices: Make Sense of Problems and Persevere in Solving Them}
Students recognize that they can solve rational equations by multiplying both sides of an equation by the same factors, as long as none of the factors are zero.

Students connect mathematical ideas and concepts when they recognize that they can use asymptotes to identify extraneous solutions.

It takes Reggie 40 minutes to wash a car by himself. It takes Angie (x) minutes to wash a car by herself. It takes Sophie five fewer minutes than Angie to wash a car. It takes 20 minutes for the three people to wash the car together. How long does it take Sophie to wash the car by herself?

Name & Time (minutes) \\
Reggie & 40 \\
Sophie & (x-5) \\

(\frac1{40}+\frac1{x-5}+\frac1x=\frac1{20})

\begin{example}{2}
\textbf{Solve a Work-Rate Problem}
Arthur and Cheyenne are making Chinese paper cutting designs to give as gifts to wish family and friends good luck in the coming year. Together they can complete the job in 24 hours. How long would it take Cheyenne to complete the job if she was working alone?
% FIG 62 838 292 1136 451
\placeholder{illustration}{Arthur, in a gold shirt, and Cheyenne, in a teal shirt, sit at a table cutting colorful paper designs with scissors. Callout above Cheyenne: Cheyenne works twice as fast as Arthur.}
Step 1 Determine the work-rates of Arthur and Cheyenne.
Let (x) represent the number of hours Arthur needs to finish the job himself.
Arthur works at a rate of (\frac{1\text{ job}}{x\text{ hours}}=\frac{\frac1x\text{ jobs}}{1\text{ hour}}).
Cheyenne is twice as fast, so she works at a rate of (\frac{1\text{ job}}{\frac12x\text{ hours}}=\frac{\frac2x\text{ jobs}}{1\text{ hour}}).
Working together, they complete the job in 24 hours, so the work-rate is (\frac{1\text{ job}}{24\text{ hours}}=\frac{\frac1{24}\text{ job}}{1\text{ hour}}).
\te{Callout: The amount of a job completed per hour is the work-rate. COMMON ERROR You might have multiplied (x) by 2 because Cheyenne works twice as fast. However, it takes Cheyenne half the time to make the gifts.}
Step 2 Write the equation for their rates working together.
(\frac1x+\frac2x=\frac1{24})
(24x(\frac1x+\frac2x)=(\frac1{24})24x)
(24+48=x)
(72=x)
It takes Arthur 72 hours to create the gifts alone. Since Cheyenne works twice as fast as Arthur, it would take her 36 hours to create the gifts alone.
\answer{Cheyenne: 36 hours. Arthur: 72 hours.}
\te{APPLICATION. Callout: Write the equation for their rates working together. Then solve.}
\end{example}

\begin{example}{3}
\textbf{Identify an Extraneous Solution}
What is the solution of the equation (\frac1{x-5}+\frac{x}{x-3}=\frac2{x^2-8x+15})?
Step 1 Multiply each side of the equation by the common denominator, ((x-5)(x-3)).
((x-5)(x-3)(\frac1{x-5}+\frac{x}{x-3})=\frac{2(x-5)(x-3)}{x^2-8x+15})
Step 2 Continue to simplify.
(\frac{(x-5)(x-3)}{x-5}+\frac{x(x-5)(x-3)}{x-3}=\frac{2(x-5)(x-3)}{x^2-8x+15})
Step 3 Divide out common factors in the numerator and the denominator.
(\frac{(x-5)(x-3)}{x-5}+\frac{x(x-5)(x-3)}{x-3}=\frac{2(x-5)(x-3)}{x^2-8x+15})
\te{Common factors in Step 3 have diagonal cancellation strokes. Callout: You can divide out common factors under the assumption that (\frac{x-5}{x-5}=1) and (\frac{x-3}{x-3}=1). This is only true if (x\neq5) or (3).}
Step 4 Solve the equation.
((x-3)+x(x-5)=2)
(x-3+x^2-5x=2)
(x^2-4x-3=2)
(x^2-4x-5=0)
((x-5)(x+1)=0)
(x=5) and (x=-1)
\te{Callout: Consider both solutions. If either solution makes the value of the denominator 0, it is not valid.}
The solution (x=5) is an extraneous solution because it is not in the domain of the original equation.
The solution of the equation (\frac1{x-5}+\frac{x}{x-3}=\frac2{x^2-8x+15}) is (-1).
To see that there is only one solution, graph (f(x)=\frac1{x-5}+\frac{x}{x-3}) and (g(x)=\frac2{x^2-8x+15}).

\begin{example}{5}
\textbf{Solve a Rate Problem}
Paddling with the current in a river, Jake traveled 16 miles. Even though he paddled upstream for an hour longer than the amount of time he paddled downstream, Jake could only travel 6 miles against the current. In still water, Jake paddles at a rate of 5 mph. What is the speed of the current in the river?
% FIG 65 799 310 1148 445
\placeholder{illustration}{Canoe with paddler on a diagonal blue river surrounded by red, orange, and yellow autumn trees. White arrow toward lower right labeled 16 miles downstream, shorter opposite arrow labeled 6 miles upstream.}
\textbf{Formulate}
Let (c) be the rate of the river's current in miles per hour.
Recall that distance = rate (\cdot) time, so time = (\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{rate}}).
(\frac{16}{5+c}+1=\frac6{5-c})
\te{Callouts: (5+c), Jake's paddle rate in still water + the rate of the current; (5-c), Jake's paddle rate in still water - the rate of the current; left side, Jake paddles 1 h longer upstream than downstream. APPLICATION.}
\textbf{Compute}
Solve the equation for (c).
(\frac{16}{5+c}+1=\frac6{5-c})
((5+c)(5-c)(\frac{16}{5+c}+1)=(\frac6{5-c})(5+c)(5-c))
(80-16c+25-c^2=30+6c)
(0=c^2+22c-75)
(0=(c+25)(c-3))
(0=c+25) (0=c-3)
(c=-25) (c=3)
\textbf{Interpret}
The solution (c=-25) is extraneous because the speed of the current cannot be negative.
The speed of the current is 3 mph.
\answer{3 mph}
\end{example}

\textbf{CONCEPT SUMMARY Solving Rational Equations}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
WORDS & A rational equation is an equation that contains a rational expression. To solve, identify the domain for the variable. Then multiply both sides of the equation by a common denominator and solve. An extraneous solution is a solution that is not valid because that value is excluded from the domain of the original equation. \\
ALGEBRA & (\frac1x+\frac2x=\frac16) \\
 & (6x(\frac1x+\frac2x)=6x(\frac16)) \\
 & (6+12=x) \\
 & (18=x) \\
 & The domain includes (x=18), so the solution to the equation is 18. \\
ALGEBRA & (\frac{x^2+4}{x-1}=\frac5{x-1}) \\
 & ((x-1)(\frac{x^2+4}{x-1})=(x-1)(\frac5{x-1})) \\
 & (x^2+4=5) \\
 & (x^2=1) \\
 & (x=\pm1) \\
 & The domain does not include (x=1), so 1 is an extraneous solution. It does include (x=-1), so the solution to the equation is (-1). \\
\end{tabular}

1. ESSENTIAL QUESTION How can you solve rational equations and identify extraneous solutions?
\answer{Begin by finding the LCD of all the terms, then multiply each term by the LCD, then simplify. 2. Vocabulary Write your own example of a rational equation that, when solved, has at least one extraneous solution.
\answer{Sample: (\frac2{x+1}-\frac1{x-1}=\frac{-2}{x^2-1}).}
