\begin{concept-box}
\textbf{Properties of Logarithms}
For positive numbers b, m, and n with (b\neq1), the following properties hold.
\begin{tabular}{ll}
Product Property of Logarithms & (\log_b mn=\log_b m+\log_b n) \\
Quotient Property of Logarithms & (\log_b\frac mn=\log_b m-\log_b n) \\
Power Property of Logarithms & (\log_b m^n=n\log_b m) \\
\end{tabular}
\end{concept-box}
\begin{example}{1}
\textbf{Prove a Property of Logarithms}
How can you prove the Product Property of Logarithms?
Let (x=\log_b m) and (y=\log_b n). Then (b^x=m) and (b^y=n).
(b^x\cdot b^y=m\cdot n)
\te{Multiply the expressions (b^x) and (b^y).}
(b^{x+y}=mn)
(x+y=\log_b mn)
\te{Rewrite the equation in logarithmic form.}
(\log_b m+\log_b n=\log_b mn)
\answer{(\log_b m+\log_b n=\log_b mn)}
\te{LEARN TOGETHER: How do you listen actively as others share ideas?}
\end{example}
\begin{tryit}{1}
Prove the Quotient Property of Logarithms.
\answer{Let (x=\log_b m) and (y=\log_b n). Then (b^x=m) and (b^y=n).
(\frac{b^x}{b^y}=\frac mn)
(b^{x-y}=\frac mn)
(x-y=\log_b(\frac mn))
(\log_b m-\log_b n=\log_b(\frac mn))}
\end{tryit}

\textbf{Write Expressions as Single Logarithms}
What is each expression written as a single logarithm?
A. (4\log_4 m+3\log_4 n-\log_4 p)
(4\log_4 m+3\log_4 n-\log_4 p)
(=\log_4(m^4)+\log_4(n^3)-\log_4 p)
(=\log_4(m^4n^3)-\log_4 p)

\begin{example}{5}
\textbf{Evaluate Logarithmic Expressions by Changing the Base}
CONCEPTUAL UNDERSTANDING
How can you use base 10 logarithms to evaluate base 2 logarithms?
To evaluate (\log_2 3) with a calculator, you need to express (\log_2 3) in terms of base 10 logarithms.
(\log_2 3=\frac{\log_2 3\cdot\log2}{\log2})
(=\frac{\log2^{\log_2 3}}{\log2})
(=\frac{\log3}{\log2})
(=\frac{0.477}{0.301}\approx1.585)
\te{Remember that exponents and logarithms are inverse operations, so they undo one another.}
\te{STUDY TIP: To divide logs with a calculator using one expression, be sure to include parentheses in the correct places. To evaluate (\log_2 3), press [LOG] [3] [divide] [(] [LOG] [2] [)] [ENTER].}
This illustrates the Change of Base Formula:
For positive numbers m, b, and a, with (b\neq1) and (a\neq1), (\log_b m=\frac{\log_a m}{\log_a b}).
\answer{(\log_2 3=\frac{\log3}{\log2}\approx1.585)}
\end{example}
