\begin{te-addendum}{0}{GeneralizeETP}
\textbf{Lesson Overview: FOCUS}
Objective. Students will be able to:
\item Use properties of exponents to rewrite expressions involving radicals in terms of rational exponents.
\item Find all real nth roots of a number.
\item Evaluate expressions with rational exponents.
\item Use nth roots to solve equations by rewriting expressions using the properties of exponents.
Essential Understanding. A rational exponent has an equivalent radical expression and any radical expression may be written with a rational exponent. This foundation is necessary to simplify expressions and solve equations.
\textbf{COHERENCE}
Previously in course, students:
\item Used theorems to find the real and complex roots of polynomial equations.
In this lesson, students:
\item Find nth roots of a number and use them to rewrite expressions and solve equations involving rational exponents.
Later in this topic, students will:
\item Use the properties of exponents and radicals to simplify radical expressions.
\textbf{RIGOR}
This lesson emphasizes a blend of conceptual understanding and procedural skill and fluency.
\item Students understand that the properties of integer exponents can be extended to rational exponents.
\item Students find the nth root of each side of an equation by raising each expression to the (\frac1n) power to solve an equation.
\end{te-addendum}

\begin{example}{1}
\textbf{Find All Real nth Roots}
A. What are all the real cube roots of 125?
An nth root of a number (c) is (x), such that (x^n=c). An nth root can be represented using a radical symbol by (x=\sqrt[n]{c}).
Since (125=5^3), (\sqrt[3]{125}=5) is a real cube root.
To determine if there are other real cube roots of 125, consider the function (f(x)=x^3-125). The zeros of (f) are the cube roots of 125.
The graph of (f) has only one (x)-intercept. Therefore, 5 is the only real cube root of 125.

Axes x,y with origin O; x labels -4,4 and grid spacing2, y labels -100,100 with grid spacing50; window approximately x=-6 to8,y=-175 to225. Red labeled points (0,-125) and (5,0).}
\answer{A. 5}
\te{MAKE SENSE AND PERSEVERE Recall that third degree polynomial functions have three solutions. Here, one solution is real and the other two are complex. How could you find the complex solutions? CONTINUED ON THE NEXT PAGE.}
% Source page 15: 228 Example 1 continued
B. What are all the real fourth roots of 16?
Since (16=2^4), one fourth root is the principal root, (\sqrt[4]{16}=2).
To determine all the real fourth roots of 16, solve (x^4=16).
(x^4=16)
(x^4-16=0)
((x^2-4)(x^2+4)=0)
((x-2)(x+2)(x^2+4)=0)
The zeros of the first two factors are 2 and (-2). The zeros of the quadratic factor (x^2+4) are imaginary. Therefore, the real fourth roots of 16 are 2 and (-2).
\answer{B. 2 and (-2).}
\te{CONSTRUCT ARGUMENTS Consider the function (f(x)=x^4-16). How can you use the graph of (f) to conclude that 16 has two real fourth roots?}
\end{example}

\begin{example}{2}
\textbf{Understand Rational Exponents}
A. What is the meaning of the exponent in the expression (16^{\frac14})?
Extend the properties of integer exponents to rational exponents.
(16^{\frac14}=(2^4)^{\frac14})
(=2^1)
(=\sqrt[4]{16})
\te{Callouts: Use the Power of a Power Property. Since (2=\sqrt[4]{16}), (16=2^4).}
Therefore, (16^{\frac14}) is another way to represent (\sqrt[4]{16}), or 2.
In general, for positive integer (n), (x^{\frac1n}) can be expressed as the radical (\sqrt[n]{x}).
(x^{\frac1n}=\sqrt[n]{x})
\te{Callout: Both the exponent (\frac1n) and the radical symbol (\sqrt[n]{x}) indicate the principal nth root. CONCEPTUAL UNDERSTANDING. STUDY TIP It is important to recognize multiple representations of the same value so you can select the form that is most efficient for a given situation.}
\answer{A. fourth root of 16; 2.}
B. Write (27^{\frac23}) as a radical.
(27^{\frac23}=(27^{\frac13})^2)
(=(\sqrt[3]{27})^2)
(=3^2)
(=9)
\te{Callout: Use the Power of a Power Property.}
This means that you can rewrite (27^{\frac23}) as ((\sqrt[3]{27})^2). Since 27 is a perfect cube, you can evaluate the radical expression mentally by taking the cube root first and then squaring.
(27^{\frac23}=(\sqrt[3]{27})^2=3^2=9)
\answer{B. ((\sqrt[3]{27})^2); 9.}
\end{example}

\begin{concept-box}
\textbf{CONCEPT Interpreting Rational Exponents}
In general, if the nth root of (c) is a real number, (\sqrt[n]{c}=c^{\frac1n}).
Furthermore, if (m) is an integer, then
(c^{\frac mn}=(c^{\frac1n})^m=(\sqrt[n]{c})^m) and (\sqrt[n]{c^m}=(c^m)^{\frac1n}=c^{\frac mn}).
\end{concept-box}

\begin{te-addendum}{4}{PurposefulQuestions}
\textbf{Simplify nth Roots. Pose Purposeful Questions}
PART A
Q: What is another approach you can use to simplify the expression?
\answer{You can write the 5th root as the rational exponent (\frac15). Then multiply the exponent of each factor by the rational exponent and simplify.}
PART B
Q: Why is it necessary to assume all variables represent positive numbers?
\answer{Because the given expression uses the radical symbol, which represents the principal (positive) root, it can only be equivalent to an expression with a nonnegative value. The exponent of (x^5) is odd, so the value of (x^5) could be negative. In this case, the expression (x^5y^2) would not be equivalent to the given expression because it would be negative. Using variables to represent only positive numbers ensures that the resulting expression is nonnegative and thus equivalent to the given expression.}

(=2m^3)
So, (\sqrt[5]{32m^{15}}=2m^3).

B. (\sqrt[4]{x^{20}y^8})
(\sqrt[4]{x^{20}y^8}=(x^{20}y^8)^{\frac14})
(=x^{\frac{20}4}y^{\frac84})
(=x^5y^2)

\textbf{CONCEPT Solving an Equation in the Form (x^n=c)}
To solve an equation in the form (x^n=c), find the nth root of both sides by raising each expression to the (\frac1n) power.
((x^n)^{\frac1n}=(c)^{\frac1n})
\end{concept-box}
\begin{example}{6}

(x=7)

The following form of radicals is called a nested radical. The number of radicals may extend to infinity.
(\sqrt{a+\sqrt{b+\sqrt{c+\sqrt{d+\ldots}}}})

1. Which equation has a solution equal to (A=\sqrt{1+\sqrt1})?
A. ((A-1)^2=1)
B. ((A^2-1)^2=1)
C. ((A^2+1)^2=1)
\answer{B}

\answer{A}
